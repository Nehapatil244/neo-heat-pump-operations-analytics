## 1. Business context
Neo Heat Pump (Sai Heating and Cooling Solutions) supplies heat pumps, heat-pump water heaters, swimming-pool heat pumps, heat-pump dryers and customer-specific solutions. Every order is **manufactured → dispatched → delivered → installed**, so the customer receives two promises: a delivery date and an installation date.

## 2. Business problem
Management lacks one reliable view of how much work is done, open or cancelled. It also cannot easily see whether delivery and installation promises are kept, which open orders are already late, and which stage of the process causes the most delay.

## 3. Objectives
1. Clean and validate the raw order export, logging every change.
2. Define KPIs with explicit numerators, denominators and exclusions.
3. Build a filterable Excel dashboard and a web dashboard from the same rules.
4. Find bottlenecks and recommend prioritised, measurable actions.

## 4. Questions investigated
- **Orders:** how many were received, completed, pending and cancelled? What is the completion rate and the current backlog?
- **Delivery:** how many deliveries were due, made, on time and delayed? Which products are delayed most?
- **Installation:** how many installations were due, completed and pending? What is the completion rate, and which stage causes the greatest delay?
- **Efficiency:** how long do fulfilment and installation take? Which orders are past their promise, and what patterns do the delays share?
- **Management:** what are the bottlenecks, which KPIs should be monitored, and how can tracking, scheduling and reporting improve?

## 6. Dataset
- **Size:** 160 orders booked between 1 Jan and 30 Jun 2026. The raw export has 163 rows because it includes 3 duplicates.
- **Mix:** 5 product families, 3 customer types and 3 regions.
- **Fields:** order, dispatch, delivery and installation dates (both promised and actual), status, delay and cancellation reasons, and an illustrative order value.
- **Personal data:** none. Customers are anonymous codes such as `CUST-4657`.

## 7. Data cleaning
The raw export contained:
- 3 duplicate rows;
- 24 inconsistent labels (for example `Complete`, `In Progress`, `Canceled`, `Pool Heat Pump`);
- 6 dates in a second format, plus an **impossible date (31-02-2026)**;
- values typed as text;
- 3 impossible date sequences.

Ten written rules fixed or flagged every one of these, and all **48 changes** are logged. Rows that could not be trusted were **kept and flagged**, and excluded only from the metric they would distort. No date was guessed and no reason was invented.
→ [Data Quality Report](analysis/Data_Quality_Report.md) · [cleaning log](data/processed/cleaning_log.csv)

## 8. KPI definitions (core)
| KPI | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Order completion rate | Completed ÷ non-cancelled orders | **80.1%** (121/151) |
| Delivery completion rate | Due & delivered ÷ deliveries due | **98.5%** (130/132) |
| On-time delivery rate | On/before promise ÷ delivered with valid dates | **72.4%** (97/134) |
| Installation completion rate | Due & installed ÷ installations due | **93.6%** (117/125) |
| On-time installation rate | On/before promise ÷ installed with valid dates | **60.2%** (71/118) |
| Overdue orders | Pending and past the promise for the current stage | **9** |
| Average installation duration | Delivery → installation | **6.7 days** |
| Order cycle time | Order → installation (completed orders) | **22.4 days** |

Cancelled orders are excluded from delivery and installation KPIs. Promises that fall after the as-of date count as *not yet due*, never overdue.
All 42 workbook KPIs are documented with numerator, denominator, exclusions, interpretation and limitations → [KPI Definitions](analysis/KPI_Definitions.md)

## 10. Key findings (from calculated results)
1. **About 1 in 4 deliveries misses its promise.** On-time delivery is 72.4%, even though 98.5% of due deliveries were made. The problem is lateness, not failure to deliver.
2. **Delays concentrate in Commercial and Custom orders.** Their on-time rates are 55.9% and 42.9%, against 90.0% for water heaters, and together they account for 23 of the 37 late deliveries. Production takes 32.9 days for Custom orders and 15.5 for Commercial, against 5.0 for water heaters.
3. **Installation is the weakest stage.** On-time installation is 60.2%, against 72.4% for delivery.
4. **24 of the 47 late installations followed an on-time delivery.** "Customer site not ready" is the reason recorded on 15 of those 24.
5. **9 orders are overdue.** 7 are waiting for installation; on average they are 38.6 days overdue, and the oldest is 81 days.
6. **Production (13.2 days) is the largest share** of the 22.4-day order cycle.
7. **Other States are on time 52.4% of the time,** against 78.5% for Bengaluru. This is a small sample.
8. **Delay reasons are often missing:** 8 of 37 late deliveries and 14 of 47 late installations have none.

Full analysis, including root-cause hypotheses and evidence levels → [Executive Summary](reports/Executive_Summary.md)

## 11. Recommendations (proposals)
| Priority | Recommendation | Metric to monitor |
|---|---|---|
| High · Low effort | **R1** Daily review of overdue and delivered-not-installed orders | Overdue orders, days overdue |
| High · Low effort | **R2** Site-readiness check 3–5 days before delivery; book the installation once the site is ready | On-time installation rate |
| High · Medium effort | **R3** Promise dates by product category, based on actual production time | On-time delivery by category |
| Medium | **R4** Component check at order acceptance · **R5** mandatory delay reasons · **R6** one validated tracker · **R7** technician calendar · **R8** weekly KPI report | See report |
| Low | **R9** Review transit allowance for Other States | On-time delivery, Other States |

## 13. How to open the Excel workbook
1. Download [`excel/operations_analysis.xlsx`](excel/operations_analysis.xlsx). On GitHub, open the file and click **Download raw file**.
2. Open it in Microsoft Excel (2010 or later). If a yellow bar appears, click **Enable Editing**.
3. Press **Ctrl + Alt + F9** once to recalculate everything.
4. Start on the **START HERE** sheet, then go to **OPERATIONS DASHBOARD**.
5. Change the yellow filter boxes to filter the dashboard. Change the yellow date in `KPI ANALYSIS!C4` to re-run every KPI for a different report date.

The workbook works in Google Sheets and LibreOffice too. Charts may look slightly different there.

## 14. How to run the web dashboard
You need Python 3.10 or later.
```bash
# 1. open a terminal in the repository folder
python -m venv .venv
# 2. activate it  (Windows: .venv\Scripts\activate   macOS/Linux: source .venv/bin/activate)
pip install -r dashboard/requirements.txt
streamlit run dashboard/app.py
```
Your browser opens at `http://localhost:8501`. To publish it free, use [Streamlit Community Cloud](https://streamlit.io/cloud) with the main file path `dashboard/app.py` (steps in the [publishing guide](docs/GitHub_Publishing_Guide.md#part-b-optional-publish-the-dashboard-online-free)).

## 15. Methodology and limitations
Raw export → cleaning (10 rules, 48-entry log) → derived columns → KPIs → Excel and Streamlit → **independent reconciliation: 175 checks, 0 differences** ([`scripts/05_qa_checks.py`](scripts/05_qa_checks.py)).

**Limitations:**
- The data is simulated, and some patterns were designed into it.
- Groups are small (14–40 timed deliveries per product).
- Reasons are self-reported.
- There is no technician, cost or customer-satisfaction data.
- "Overdue" depends on the 30-Jun-2026 as-of date.
- PivotTables and slicers are not included, because the generator cannot create them. The workbook uses formula summaries and dropdown filters instead, with steps for adding a real PivotTable.

→ [Project Methodology](docs/Project_Methodology.md)
